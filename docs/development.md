# Development

Technical notes on Kennste?!. How to set up and run the app is in the [README](../README.md). The app's interface is in German; UI terms are quoted in German below, with a translation where it helps.

## Features in detail

- **Music quiz** with six game modes, offered as four games with variants (`src/lib/game/modes/groups.ts`): **Buzzer** (Reihum = Buzzer-Quiz, Alle gegen alle), **Zeitstrahl** (Jeder für sich, Im Team = the timeline with `coop`, Alle gleichzeitig = Zeitstrahl für alle), **Jahr schätzen** and **Vier Antworten** (Titel, Interpret, Abwechselnd = the `ask` setting). A variant is a mode, optionally with some of its settings fixed; the start page shows the four games (a tap creates the game with the first variant), the lobby the variant as segments (phone) or cards (laptop) with one sentence on what makes it different, and everywhere else the mode is named with its variant (`modeName`, e.g. “Zeitstrahl · Im Team”; kept in `game.evening` per game). The TV lobby shows “Gleich spielen wir” with the variant, its sentence and the rules. The host logs in with Spotify and creates a game; players join by QR code, link or code without logging in. The music plays on the host's Spotify device.
  - **Buzzer-Quiz** (Buzzer · Reihum): Players take turns putting on a song (search or browse; never from a playlist), the others buzz, and whoever put it on judges. “Keine Ahnung – überspringen” doesn't lock the buzzer either.
  - **Alle gegen alle** (Buzzer · Alle gegen alle, everyone vs. everyone): Everyone hears the same song from the playlist, anyone can buzz – once per song: whoever buzzed has seen the solution. “Keine Ahnung” (no idea) only tells the others; the buzzer stays open. Whoever buzzes says the answer out loud first (time to answer, “Gesagt – Lösung zeigen” ends it early) and only then sees the solution – as the only one, through `/api/game/[id]/answer` (it isn't part of the game state; the route only returns it in the `judging` state) – and judges themselves (no verdict within 20 s, `JUDGE_MS`, counts as wrong). Wrong: the song keeps playing. Right: everyone sees the solution, the music keeps playing, and the others vote without a time limit; if more than half of them confirm, the point counts, once that's out of reach, it doesn't. If the vote gets stuck, the host decides (“Als Host: Punkt geben / Kein Punkt”). The song is revealed by itself when everyone buzzed or said “Keine Ahnung”, or the time is up.
  - **Zeitstrahl** (timeline): Players take turns hearing an unknown song from the playlist and placing it in their timeline by release year. Placed right = card won. The others see live which gap is being tapped; optionally with a time limit. Variant “Im Team” (as a team): one timeline for the whole team, the others suggest gaps, every wrong card costs a life (1–5).
  - **Zeitstrahl für alle** (Zeitstrahl · Alle gleichzeitig, timeline for everyone): One shared timeline with one open start card. A song from the playlist plays, everyone taps a gap at the same time, face down (every choice goes to the server right away and counts when the time is up; “Fertig” reveals earlier). Those who are right score a point; the card joins the timeline. Before the reveal, the TV and the players only see who is done; afterwards every choice in its gap.
  - **Jahr schätzen** (guess the year): A song from the playlist plays, everyone guesses the release year at the same time (slider, ±1/±10, decades). The year set goes to the server as a draft and counts when the time is up; “Fertig” reveals earlier once everyone is done. Spot on 3 points, up to 2 years off 2, up to 5 years off 1. The reveal shows all guesses on a number line.
  - **Song-Tipp** (Vier Antworten, four answers): A song from the playlist plays, everyone picks the right title or artist out of four answers at the same time (the variant: title, artist or alternating; wrong answers from the same playlist, the least offered first, songs that still come up in this game count as offered twice more). The first answer counts; right 2 points, the first to be right +1.
- **Just Playing** — shows the song currently playing on Spotify with device, progress and genres.

- Goal: a fixed number of rounds or “best of” (first to N points)
- Song source: follows from the mode (`songSourceOf` in `reducer.ts`, not a setting): in the buzzer quiz the players pick the songs, every other mode plays a playlist (shuffled; search by name or Spotify link, suggestions as search terms: mixed ones like “Hitster” first – searched right away, songs from every decade make the best rounds – then by decade and genre, more results on request; playlists with fewer than 50 songs come after the bigger ones; the host's own playlists come last and folded, because they know the songs)
- No song comes twice in a game; once the playlist is used up, the game ends (“Playlist durchgespielt”). Across games – a rematch, the same playlist picked again, a later lobby of the same host – songs come in this order: new ones, then those heard in the lobby, then those played before, the longest ago first (`freshFirst` in `store.ts`); a foreign playlist reads further blocks as long as fewer than 30 new songs are left. Which songs a host's games played is kept per host for 180 days (`src/lib/playedSongs.ts`, at most 5000 per host, hosts only as a hash of their login), with `DATA_DIR` set in `DATA_DIR/played-songs.json` (the Docker image uses `/data`), otherwise until the next restart
- Hints (cover, title, artist, year), each can be switched on
- Time limits for answering and for revealing; one buzz per player and round. When the time is up, the server ends the round itself (`deadline` in the mode, `scheduleDeadline` in `store.ts`, sends `timeout` as the host) – phones stop timers as soon as the tab is in the background. What's currently selected counts (year, tapped gap). In the buzzer quiz, “Auflösen nach” (reveal after) reveals the song, as does nobody being able to guess anymore (everyone skipped or out of buzzes)
- Random starting point instead of the intro (all modes)
- Timers only start once Spotify really plays the song; pause/play resumes instead of starting over
- Buzzer quiz with songs picked by players: after the reveal, every phone shows the song for a moment (whoever guessed it highlighted at the top; title and artist below the cover), after 10 s it moves on to the next player's song search by itself (`AUTO_NEXT_MS`, `revealedAt`, via the mode's deadline; “Jetzt Song suchen” goes right away). While picking, the last song is a strip at the top, on the laptop next to the search, on the TV at the bottom
- Otherwise (playlist, other modes): after the reveal, the player who's up next continues (the host can skip ahead in the menu). Their continue button is fixed at the bottom of the phone (“Du bist dran”, a short vibration); the others can press “Weiter geht's!” (key W, can be taken back; action `voteContinue`, votes in `game.continueVote`). If more than half of them want to continue, a 5 s countdown runs for everyone, then the server continues by itself (`scheduleContinue` in `store.ts`, as the player who's up). They see who wants to continue and get a short vibration; the TV shows “Lea will weitermachen” and the countdown
- Finding songs (buzzer, songs picked by players): “Stöbern” (browse, the default) by decade and genre (`/api/game/[id]/browse`, Spotify search with `year:`/`genre:` filters, `src/lib/browse.ts`, starting at a random page among the most popular songs) or “Suchen” (search); also “Zufälliger Song daraus” (a random song from it, `/random`). Swiping sideways switches between them. Playlists aren't a source, because in Development Mode the app can only read the host's own
- A queue to line up songs before your own turn; the song search loads more results on scroll (10 per page, `offset`) and keeps search term and selection per game and player (`localStorage`), even if a round starts in between
- Viewer role, removing players
- Set up music before the game (`/setup`): explains that everyone plays in one room, shows what plays where, pick a device and play a test song; skipped if music already plays on a controllable device
- Pick the playback device (Spotify Connect): during setup, in the lobby (music row) or during the game in the host's game menu; “Automatisch” uses the currently active device
- Lobby: the host sets up what's played in one card (mode, the rules – length, time limit and what changes the game most first –, songs: players or playlist – a missing playlist is pointed out there and below “Spiel starten”), manages players by tapping their chip (let them watch, remove them) and invites through the code at the top (QR code, link, TV view; always visible on the laptop). Players only see what's played, who's in and whom they're waiting for (`GameLobby.tsx`, `LobbyGame.tsx`)
- Music in the lobby (the host's row, `LobbyMusic.tsx`): what's playing (play/pause), behind “…” the device, “Musik abspielen” (search a song or playlist, `POST /api/game/[id]/music`). In the lobby, the playback state includes title and cover, during a game it doesn't (it would give the song away)
- The TV lobby shows “Läuft gerade” (now playing); the host can switch the TV to a full-screen view of it right next to “TV-Ansicht verbunden” in the lobby (`screenView`, “Lobby | Läuft gerade”)
- Logo splash (`Splash.tsx`): when the game starts or everyone is back in the lobby, the “?!” logo pops up on phones, laptop and TV for a moment (1.5 s, a tap skips it; only for a change the open page sees – not after reloading)
- Start countdown (`StartCountdown` in `Splash.tsx`): “Spiel starten” sends `countdown`; all devices count down 5 s in the logo (`game.countdown.endsAt`), the host can cancel (`cancelCountdown`), settings are locked meanwhile. Meanwhile the server reads the songs and checks the first release years (`prepareStart`, progress in `countdown.songs`/`years`) and starts by itself once the time is up and everything is ready (`scheduleStart` in `store.ts`, sends `start` as the host)
- Songs of the evening (`EveningSongs.tsx`): when everyone is back in the lobby, the game's songs move to `game.evening` (each song once). The lobby shows them as a card; the host saves them as one private playlist (`/api/game/[id]/save-playlist`), songs of later games are added to the same playlist (`savedPlaylist.songs` counts how many are in it already)
- End of the game (`End.tsx`): winner and podium, awards (`awards.ts`, per mode from the game's history, at most five, only for real achievements, ties share) and all songs with cover and who guessed them (a tap opens Spotify). Below the songs, the host sees that the evening's songs can be saved in the lobby. On the phone as tabs (Endstand, Auszeichnungen, Songs – swipe sideways to switch), on the laptop side by side, on the TV one after another every 10 s
- The host can cancel a running game: everyone stays in the lobby, scores are reset. Back in the lobby (cancelled or "Neues Spiel" after the end) the music plays on (`continueInLobby` in `store.ts`): a round's song still playing plays to its end, then the game's playlist takes over instead of the rest of the album; with nothing playing, the playlist starts right away. Music the host picks in the lobby stays
- Closing the lobby (host's game menu, `DELETE /api/game/[id]`, `closeGame` in `store.ts`): once a game was played, the game isn't deleted but closed (action `close`, `game.closedAt`; a running game is ended and joins the evening, the music stops). Everyone – also on the TV, also after reloading – gets the evening's summary (`EveningEnd.tsx`): every game with its mode, songs and top three (`game.evening.games`, team modes won/lost), who won the most games, the songs of the evening; the host can still save them as a playlist. Nothing else can be done in a closed game, nobody can join, and the host can start a new game right away. It's cleaned up like any idle game after 2 h; a TV connected via `/tv` shows a pairing code again after 2 minutes. Without any game played, closing deletes the game as before
- Stage light in the cover's color (style “Bühne”): as soon as a round's song is dealt – or in the lobby, whatever plays – the server reads its cover from Spotify's image server (`coverColor.ts`, JPEG via `jpeg-js`, only `*.scdn.co`/`*.spotifycdn.com`, cached, the next song's read ahead), works out the main color (`accentOf` in `accentColor.ts`, shared with “Just Playing”) and sends only the color (`game.stageColor`) – the song stays hidden. Every screen of the game glows in it (`StageLight` context, `Decor`) and fades over to the next song's color (`@property --decor-tint`); grey or dark covers keep the normal light
- Brand: logo (`components/ui/Logo.tsx`: the “?!” tile from the app icon plus the word mark) at the top of the start and TV pages, favicon (`app/favicon.ico`, `app/icon.png`) from the same tile. The start page and the host's game menu show the version and a link to the source code (`components/BuildInfo.tsx`, `lib/version.ts`): a release just “v1.2.3”, images in between “v1.2.3 · edge abc1234” or “· pr-42 abc1234” (build argument `APP_BUILD` from CI), locally “· dev”; `/api/health` returns both
- Like an app on the phone: menus (bottom sheets, `components/ui/Sheet.tsx`) also close by swiping down (at the handle and title, anywhere if the content doesn't scroll) and with the back gesture – every open menu adds a browser history entry (`pushState`, supported by Next.js); a menu opened right after another one waits for that one's step back first. Long menus scroll instead of squeezing their buttons. Tabs switch by swiping sideways (`components/ui/useSwipeTabs.ts`). Can be added to the home screen (`app/manifest.ts`, icons in `public/`, `app/apple-icon.png`; template: Bricolage “?!” in the Party colors), then full screen. No double-tap zoom, no rubber-band or pull-to-refresh scrolling, buttons without text selection
- Laptop/PC layout from 1024 px width with keyboard shortcuts (buzzer: space to buzz, S skip, P play/pause, 1–4 hints, R/F judge, A reveal; timeline: ←/→ pick a gap, Enter place, N another song; Enter = continue, W = “Weiter geht's!”)
- Player cards on the TV (`PlayerCards` in `modes/allplay.tsx`, `playerCards: true` in the mode UI): in Alle gegen alle, Jahr schätzen, Song-Tipp and Zeitstrahl für alle, big cards with points and the state of every player (buzzes left, guessed/thinking, vote; after the reveal guess, answer and points of the round) sit at the bottom instead of a score column – the round gets the full width. Flat for Zeitstrahl für alle, so the timeline has room
- TV view for TV/projector (`/game/<code>/screen`): without joining, via “TV-Ansicht” next to the code field, in the lobby, or offered automatically to viewers on a big screen; the lobby shows when a TV view is connected. It's laid out for 1920×1080 and scaled to the screen with CSS `zoom` (`useTvScale`, also on `/tv`), so it never scrolls – many TVs report 1280×720 or 960×540. From 900 px width (landscape) and on a TV paired via `/tv` it's the TV view, below that the phone layout
- Connect a TV without typing (`/tv`): the TV shows a 4-digit pairing code and a QR code (valid for 10 minutes, renews itself, single use). The host enters it in the lobby (player list, invite sheet) or, once the game runs, in the game menu under “TV verbinden” (`POST /api/game/[id]/screen`, player token) or scans the QR code (`/tv/pair/<code>`, recognized by the Spotify login via `/api/tv/pair`). The TV polls every 2 s (`/api/tv/<screenId>`, a secret ID instead of the code) and jumps into the game; once the game is gone (or 2 minutes after the lobby was closed) back to `/tv` (marker in `sessionStorage`). At most 10 wrong codes per game and minute, at most 100 open codes (`src/lib/game/pairing.ts`)
- Watching on a phone without joining: the same page shows a phone layout below 1024 px (lobby with “Mitspielen”, the running round with scores, end of the game); on the phone the entry points are called “Zuschauen”, and these viewers don't count as a connected TV view

## Running locally

Create a Spotify app as in the README, but with the redirect URI `http://127.0.0.1:3000/api/auth/callback/spotify`. Then:

```bash
cp .env.example .env.local    # NEXTAUTH_URL=http://127.0.0.1:3000
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open the app at [http://127.0.0.1:3000](http://127.0.0.1:3000) – Spotify no longer allows `localhost` (`src/proxy.ts` redirects). Players on the same Wi-Fi open `http://<computer's IP>:3000` and enter the game code; locally, the QR code points to `127.0.0.1`.

## Tests

Which kind of test goes where, and the rules for them, is in `AGENTS.md` (“Tests”, in German). The building blocks:

- **Unit tests** for the game logic: the reducer and the modes are pure functions; tests build game states with `applyAction`.
- **Render tests** (`renderToStaticMarkup`): what every role sees.
- **happy-dom tests** with Testing Library: clicks, timers (`vi.useFakeTimers`), the event stream (a rebuilt `EventSource`).
- **Spotify simulator** (a stateful fake instead of individual mocks) and **scenario tests** for whole flows.
- **Spotify diagnosis** as a cross-check against the real Spotify (a contract test, run by hand, because it needs an account and a running device).
- **Playwright** (`e2e/`): the real app (production build) in Chromium with several browsers at once – host on a phone, players, TV – against the simulator as an HTTP server (`e2e/spotify-server.mts`). The app gets other addresses for that (`SPOTIFY_API_URL`, `SPOTIFY_ACCOUNTS_URL`, `MUSICBRAINZ_URL`) and a test login instead of Spotify (only with `E2E_TEST_LOGIN=1` and `SPOTIFY_API_URL` set). First run: `npx playwright install chromium`.

**Spotify simulator** (`src/test/spotifySim.ts`): mimics the Web API the app uses, with state (catalog, own/foreign playlists, devices including idle ones, playback with album/playlist context, expiring access including token refresh, queue with autoplay after the end, shuffle; with `lag`, shuffle and play/pause take effect late like in the real apps). The scenario tests in `src/lib/game/scenarios.test.ts` play whole flows with it; `timing.scale = 0` (`src/lib/spotify.ts`) turns the waiting off. Only the diagnosis shows how the real Spotify behaves:

**Spotify diagnosis** (`/diagnose`, logged in, `src/lib/diagnose.ts`): checks with one click, with your own account and a device of your choice, what the game expects from Spotify – start a song through its album (with the time until it's reported), start at a given position, pause/resume, read your own playlist, the queue from position 21 with shuffle (order compared with the playlist), shuffle and the previous music restored afterwards, read a foreign playlist block by block, search songs by decade (and how many Spotify returns per page), every genre of the song browser alone and with the 80s, candidate genre names for German music of the 80s (`german pop` finds none there), save songs as a playlist (into a reused test playlist “Kennste?! – Diagnose”). It plays music for a moment and restores the previous state afterwards. The diagnosis itself is tested against the simulator.

## Architecture

```
src/lib/game/
  types.ts           Types shared by client and server (Game, Action, GameEvent, …)
  reducer.ts         Core game logic: lobby, roles, order, goal, playlist, playback
  modes/             Game modes (pure logic): their own settings, round state, actions
    buzzer.ts        Buzzer-Quiz
    versus.ts        Alle gegen alle (everyone at once, the solution only for whoever buzzed)
    timeline.ts      Zeitstrahl
    together.ts      Zeitstrahl für alle (everyone at once: `simultaneous`)
    year.ts          Jahr schätzen (everyone at once: `simultaneous`)
    choice.ts        Song-Tipp (everyone at once)
    index.ts         Registry of all modes
  awards.ts          Awards and song list for the end, from the game's history (`game.history`, written per round by the modes via `turn.record`)
  store.ts           In-memory state, player tokens, host token refresh, live updates, Spotify polling, server timers
  auth.ts            Token check for route handlers
  pairing.ts         Connecting a TV: pairing codes, limiting wrong attempts
  client.ts          Typed API client for the browser
  useGameSession.ts  React hook: subscribe to the event stream, send actions
src/lib/spotify.ts   Spotify Web API helpers
src/lib/playedSongs.ts  Songs a host's games played, across lobbies (in memory, a JSON file in DATA_DIR)
src/components/game/
  GameView.tsx       Game UI for every role (lobby, round, result, host menu)
  GameLobby.tsx      Lobby (LobbyGame.tsx: mode, rules, songs; LobbyMusic.tsx: music row; EveningSongs.tsx: songs of the evening)
  Watch.tsx          Watching on a phone without joining (the TV view page below 1024 px)
  modes/             UI part of the game modes (round, settings, rules)
src/components/MusicSetup.tsx  “Musik einrichten” (uses /api/spotify/player, /search, /play)
src/app/
  page.tsx           Start: login, Just Playing, create/join a game
  now-playing/       Just Playing
  setup/             Set up music before the game (device, test song)
  game/[id]/         The game – for host and players
  tv/                Connect a TV (pairing code), and tv/pair/[code] after the QR scan
```

**Adding a game mode:** `src/lib/game/modes/<mode>.ts` (implements `GameMode`, including `view`: what a device may see before the reveal – the song, the others' guesses), register it in `modes/index.ts`, add its data type to the `ModeData` union and offer it in `modes/groups.ts` (as a game of its own or as a variant); the UI goes under `src/components/game/modes/<mode>/` (implements `ModeUI`: round, TV view, watching on a phone, settings for the lobby card, rules) and is registered in `components/game/modes/index.ts`.

- **Actions** all go through `POST /api/game/[id]/action`. The reducer checks who may do what (host, player whose turn it is, guesser, viewer).
- **Authentication:** every player gets a secret token on joining (header `X-Player-Token`, kept in `sessionStorage` in the browser). The host's Spotify credentials never leave the server and are refreshed automatically.
- **Live updates** via server-sent events (`GET /api/game/[id]/events`): game state and playback state. Spotify is polled once every 3 s per game, not per client.
- **Each device gets its own view** (`src/lib/game/view.ts`): the server keeps the full game, but every response and stream sends what that player (token header, on the stream `?token=` since EventSource can't send headers) or a spectator (TV, no token) may see. Each mode's `view` removes what isn't revealed yet: the song (in the buzzer quiz only whoever put it on knows it, the others get the hints given so far), the others' years, answers and gaps (who answered stays, as a placeholder). The running song's Spotify ID is replaced by a stand-in (HMAC with a key per game) in the game state and in the playback state, so the playback bar still matches it but nobody can look it up; “play it again” sends the stand-in, the server maps it back (`unmask` in `store.ts`). Nothing to hide outside a game.
- **Docker image** (amd64 + arm64): dependencies and the build run natively on the build machine's platform; for arm64 the result is only copied (no emulation). That works because the build is pure JavaScript except for `sharp`; `sharp` (image optimization for `next/image`) is removed in the Dockerfile and `images.unoptimized` is set. **So don't use `next/image`** – or adapt the Dockerfile first.
- **Health checks and shutdown:** `/api/health` (the server responds, with the version; Docker's `HEALTHCHECK` uses it). On SIGTERM/SIGINT, Next.js waits for open requests – but the event streams never end by themselves, so the container would hang until SIGKILL. `src/instrumentation.ts` therefore registers `beginShutdown` (`src/lib/lifecycle.ts`), which closes all streams; the browsers reconnect by themselves. Measured: stopping with an open stream takes 0.02 s instead of hanging until SIGKILL. Running games are lost on restart (state in memory).
- **CI/CD** (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`): lint, typecheck, unit and E2E tests; in parallel the image job builds the image, starts it as a test (smoke test: the healthcheck must turn “healthy”), scans it with Trivy (critical vulnerabilities with an available fix stop it) and builds both platforms into the build cache. Only when all of that is green does the publish job push exactly that image (from the cache; a release is told by the merged release PR's commit, so it isn't rebuilt either) with SBOM and provenance, signed with cosign (keyless). Actions are pinned to commit SHAs; Dependabot (`.github/dependabot.yml`) proposes weekly updates for npm, actions and the Node image; major versions that don't work yet (ESLint 10, TypeScript 7, `@types/node` beyond the Node version of the image) are ignored there, with the reason next to each. To check workflows: [`actionlint`](https://github.com/rhysd/actionlint).
- **Test images for pull requests:** every PR builds and checks the image too (smoke test, Trivy). PRs from this repo publish it as `ghcr.io/mg500/kennste:pr-<number>` (signed), to try a branch on the server before merging; `edge`, `latest` and the versions stay untouched. PRs from forks and from Dependabot only have read access and publish nothing. Nobody cleans up old `pr-*` images yet (delete them in the package settings if needed).
- **Releases** with [release-please](https://github.com/googleapis/release-please): from `feat:`/`fix:` commits it maintains a release pull request with the version and `CHANGELOG.md` (`feat` → minor, `fix` → patch, `!`/`BREAKING CHANGE` → major; `refactor`, `test`, `docs`, `ci`, `chore` don't show up). Merging it creates the tag, the GitHub release and the image tags `1.2.3`, `1.2`, `1` and `latest`; every other commit on `main` lands as `edge`. To force a version: `Release-As: 2.0.0` in the commit message. Needed once: enable “Allow GitHub Actions to create and approve pull requests” under *Settings → Actions → General*. The release PR itself triggers no CI (it's created with `GITHUB_TOKEN`) – it only changes the version and the changelog. Commit messages and PR titles are in English (until October 2026 they were German, so older changelog entries may be too).
- **State** lives in memory. Exactly one instance runs; a restart ends running games. Games are deleted after 2 h of inactivity. Only the songs played per host are written to a file (`DATA_DIR`), so later games bring new ones first.

## Design decisions

- **State in memory, one instance – no Redis, no database.** The one exception is which songs a host's games played (so a playlist played again brings new songs first): a small JSON file, written a few seconds after a change and on shutdown – no database needed for one list per host. A game lives for one evening; losing it on a restart is acceptable, and the server is the only clock (timers, deadlines, Spotify polling per game). A second instance would need shared state *and* exactly one owner per game for its timers and Spotify polling – e.g. games pinned to an instance by code (sticky routing) or Redis with a lock per game. That's a lot of moving parts for a party game, so it's deliberately left out; one small container serves many simultaneous games.
- **A Spotify simulator instead of mocks.** Most bugs were in the interplay with Spotify (delays, devices, queues, shuffle), not in single calls. `src/test/spotifySim.ts` simulates a Spotify account with devices and playback state, and is used by unit scenarios and, as an HTTP server, by the E2E tests. It only models behavior that was observed on the real Spotify (via `/diagnose`), so tests don't pass against an imagined API.

## Notes on the Spotify API

- `/audio-features` is blocked (403) for new apps (created after Nov. 2024).
- `preview_url` is mostly `null` for new apps — so the preview for players rarely kicks in.
- Playlists: in Development Mode, `/playlists/{id}/items` only returns your own or collaborative playlists. The app reads foreign playlists through the playback queue: start the playlist at position N, read the queue (current song + 20), pause; shuffle is off meanwhile. When picking a playlist, one block from a random position is read, then the playlist plays on in the lobby from the block read last – or, for a short playlist or one whose length Spotify doesn't tell, right where reading it stopped (own playlists, read without the queue, start at a random song – picking a playlist always starts it); songs that play in the lobby – including the one playing right at the start, and the one that plays for a moment while a block is read – come last in the game (`heardLast` in `store.ts`), so nobody knows them from before. A new round always starts its song from the beginning, even if Spotify has it loaded already (only the play button resumes). More is read at the start of a game (during the countdown) until there are 30 unplayed songs, and before every “Weiter” if fewer than 3 are left (`loadMoreSongs` in `store.ts`, `src/lib/playlists.ts`). The music on the device stops for a moment meanwhile. Right after the start, a device may hand out only part of the queue (a Pixel 9 once returned 4 songs instead of 21, the run before all of them): the app reads it up to 4 times, 0.5 s apart, until the block is complete – otherwise the songs up to the next block would never be read. The Spotify apps apply shuffle and report play/pause only a moment after the command (seen with the diagnosis on a Mac) – so the app waits with `waitForPlayer` until shuffle is really off, and checks again after starting the playlist. Also, the Mac app starts a playlist one song after the requested position (although that song is playable): the blocks therefore overlap by one song (`BLOCK_STRIDE`), and at the playlist's end one song less is taken over, so no autoplay song slips into the game – at most the first or last song of the playlist goes unused.
- Playing: the Spotify apps (Mac, phone, TV) accept a single song via `uris` with 204, but then play nothing (as of Oct. 2026, only the Web Player plays it). Songs are therefore started through their album (`context_uri` + `offset`); the app sometimes plays another version (e.g. without “Remastered”), which counts as the same song by its normalized title (`sameTrack`). After the song, the album keeps playing – so during a game the server pauses right before the round's song ends (from the playback state it polls), and pauses anyway if it sees that Spotify moved on after the song played (`watchSongEnd` in `store.ts`, logged as `songEnded`).
- Search: by year alone (`year:1980-1989`), Spotify returns only 5 songs per page although 10 are asked for (seen with the diagnosis, Oct 2026; with a genre it's 10), and its `total` is useless there (5, although later pages bring new songs) – so the song search and browsing page on until a page comes back empty, not until one is short.
- Genres are loaded from the artist endpoint and are empty for many artists.
- **Release years:** Spotify's album date is often a remaster (2011 instead of 1975). The server finds the year of the first release through [MusicBrainz](https://musicbrainz.org/doc/MusicBrainz_API) – by ISRC, otherwise by search for the title's words (the earliest year among the hits with the same title *and* artist; spaces and punctuation don't count, so “Solang man …” finds “So lang’ man …”). MusicBrainz allows 1 request/s, so for playlists this runs in the background, current and next songs first (`enrichYears`); a round still waits up to 5 s for its song to be checked (`verifyUpcoming`, at the start also for the start cards of the timeline modes, `startCards` in the mode). These requests go before the background check's, and every request has a 4 s time limit – a hanging MusicBrainz holds nothing up. Songs picked by players (buzzer quiz) are checked after playing; the round gets the corrected year through `trackUpdated`. If MusicBrainz can't answer (offline, 503 for too many requests), the year doesn't count as checked and is asked for again later (up to 3 passes, 30 s apart). Optionally set `MUSICBRAINZ_CONTACT` (contact in the user agent “Kennste/<version> (…)”, otherwise the `NEXTAUTH_URL`).
- **How long a new song takes:** for the start of a game (at the end of the countdown – the songs are usually there by then) and every song change, the server writes a JSON line to the log (`docker logs`), e.g. `{"event":"songChange","game":"ABCD","action":"next","ms":{"songs":0,"years":850,"play":1420},"totalMs":2270}` – `songs`: reading more of a foreign playlist, `years`: checking release years, `play`: starting the song (including waking the device and checking). That shows what slows things down on a real device (`src/lib/log.ts`).
